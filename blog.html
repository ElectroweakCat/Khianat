#12 entry (18.07.26)

Housekeeping: made the Impressum leaner and more privacy friendly. A small hobby chess site does not need to publish more personal data than the law actually asks for. The privacy policy got the same trim. Nothing else changed, back to chess soon!

#11 entry (12.06.26)

Quality of life round! There is finally a New game button (the README promised one for years, oops), and the last move gets highlighted on the board so you can actually see what Khianat just played. Also cleaned up the GitHub side of things: fresh README, updated license, and the site now links to the source code in the footer. Go have a look at the repo if you are curious how the sausage is made.

#10 entry (14.03.26)

The About page grew up! It is now a proper little tech documentation: how the engine works from move generation over evaluation to the alpha-beta search, with diagrams of the search tree, the pruning, the knight table as a heatmap and an Elo history chart. All hand-drawn in SVG and CSS, no chart library needed. Probably the longest page on the site now...

#9 entry (11.01.26)

Small one: every move now makes a proper click sound, like a piece being set down on a real board. Had the sample lying around forever. Feels so much better!

#8 entry (26.05.25)

Emergency fix! Right after leaving the opening book, pieces suddenly jumped around like crazy and at some point I was playing Black?! Turns out: when the thinking time ran out, the search aborted with an exception and skipped taking back its trial moves, so half a calculated line just stayed on the board. Now the abort runs through a simple flag and every undo is guaranteed to happen. Stable again, phew.

#7 entry (03.05.25)

The big one: Khianat is a real engine now! The old Minimax plan is finally implemented: alpha-beta pruning, about 3 seconds of thinking per move, quiescence search, and the piece square tables actually in use. Mate, stalemate and draws are scored correctly, and the opening book stayed of course. My guess: somewhere around 1500 Elo. I honestly struggle against it myself now, especially when I get impatient... Next up maybe a transposition table and a smarter evaluation.

#6 entry (27.01.24)

Fresh paint! One central stylesheet, card design, and the site finally works on the phone: the board resizes with the screen. Also slimmed down the endless copy-paste Impressum and said goodbye to the bouncing balls. RIP, little guys.

#5 entry (19.04.23)

Khianat can talk now! It says "Yes" when it checkmates you and a grumpy "No" when you win. And the level survey finally counts for real: a small PHP script stores the votes and shows the percentages after you answer. Be honest about your level ;)

#4 entry (08.10.22)

Big bugfix round. The site was loading chess.js from a folder that doesn't even exist, so the board ignored every single move... embarrassing. Fixed that, repaired the dead navigation links, the duplicated radio buttons and a bunch of invalid HTML. Khianat is playable again!

#3 entry (26.05.22)

Khianat is not so very stupid anymore! Now it is maximizing the losses of the opponents, while completely ignoring the consequences. This is similar to a tree search algorithm with depth 1.

#2 entry (24.05.22)

I am failing to implement a working evaluation function for a given position on the chessboard. This evaluation function would be the basis of a Monte Carlo tree search algorithm. Nevertheless, I gave Khianat some rudimentary opening theory knowledge.

#1 entry (08.05.22)

Today is the day my first files get uploaded to the server and finally the first content is visible online! The implementation of the chess engine is sadly not yet done and there are some issues to fix. For now the computer just makes random legal moves. Should be not too hard to win ;)
